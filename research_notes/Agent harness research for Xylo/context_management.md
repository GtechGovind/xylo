# Context Window Management, Long-Context Degradation and Context Compaction for LLM Agents (harness-level techniques for Xylo)

Scope note for the report writer: all arXiv IDs below were checked against the arXiv API (export.arxiv.org) on 2026-10-31; titles, authors and venue comments are taken from that metadata. Numbers come from the paper abstracts or full-text pages fetched during this research. Many 2026 items are recent preprints that have not been peer reviewed, and their model names (e.g., "GPT-5.5", "Claude Opus 4.8", "Qwen3.8") are as the papers report them. Each per-paper bullet ends with a "Harness note", which is my inference about how Xylo could apply the result. These notes are not claims from the source.

## Q1. How does model performance degrade as context grows, and what does that imply for how much context to keep and where to place important information?

### Takeaway
Every serious benchmark from 2023 to 2026 finds that quality falls as input length grows, well before the advertised window is full. Retrieval failure is not the only cause: length alone hurts, distractors hurt, and multi-turn accumulation hurts. Newer frontier models show less of the classic "lost in the middle" U-curve, but the length penalty remains. A harness should treat the usable ("effective") window as much smaller than the nominal one, especially for local 4B–30B models. It should keep context minimal and high-signal, and put the current objective near the end of the prompt (recency) while keeping stable instructions at the top (for caching).

### Cited Findings

**Foundational position/length studies (2023–2024)**
- **Lost in the Middle: How Language Models Use Long Contexts**: Liu, Lin, Hewitt, Paranjape et al.; arXiv 2307.03172; TACL. Performance is highest when relevant information is at the beginning or end of the input and falls sharply in the middle, "even for explicitly long-context models." With GPT-3.5-Turbo and 20 documents, multi-doc QA accuracy is 75.8% when the answer document is first, 53.8% when it is in the middle (index 9) and 63.2% when it is last. Closed-book accuracy (no documents) is 56.1%, so a mid-context answer did worse than no context. Extended-context variants (16K/100K) did not clearly beat their standard counterparts. Query-aware contextualization (placing the query both before and after the data) made key-value retrieval near-perfect. Going from 20 to 50 retrieved docs added only about 1.5% (GPT-3.5) and about 1% (Claude-1.3); reader accuracy saturates long before retriever recall does. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03172); [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.03172v3)
  - Harness note: do not let the key instruction or task statement drift into the middle of a long history. Re-state it near the end (e.g., a pinned "current objective / todo" block appended just before the latest turn). Retrieving more chunks has quickly diminishing returns.
- **RULER: What's the Real Context Size of Your Long-Context Language Models?**: Hsieh, Sun, Kriman, Acharya et al. (NVIDIA); arXiv 2404.06654; COLM 2024. Models score near-perfect on vanilla needle-in-a-haystack (NIAH) but show "large performance drops as the context length increases" on multi-needle, multi-hop tracing and aggregation tasks. Of 17 models claiming 32K+ context, only half stay satisfactory at 32K. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06654)
  - The RULER leaderboard defines *effective* context length using a threshold of 85.6% (Llama-2-7B's 4K score). Effective lengths relevant to local models: Llama3.1-8B claims 128K, effective 32K (87.4 at 32K, 77.0 at 128K). Qwen3-8B (self-reported) claims 128K, effective 64K (77.4 at 128K). Qwen3-4B (self-reported) is effective at 64K (66.0 at 128K). Phi3-mini claims 128K, effective 32K. Mistral-7B-v0.2 claims 32K, effective 16K (13.8 at 128K). Mistral-Nemo claims 128K, effective 16K. Llama3.1-70B is effective at 64K (66.6 at 128K). GPT-4-1106 claims 128K, effective 64K. Gemini-1.5-pro is >128K. Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-1M (self-reported) is >128K. — [RULER GitHub leaderboard](https://github.com/NVIDIA/RULER)
  - Harness note: keep a per-model "effective context" table that defaults to RULER-style effective length, not the advertised `n_ctx`, and size the compaction trigger from it.
- **Large Language Models Can Be Easily Distracted by Irrelevant Context**: Shi, Chen, Misra, Scales et al.; arXiv 2302.00093; ICML 2023. On GSM-IC (grade-school math with irrelevant sentences), accuracy is "dramatically decreased" when irrelevant information is included. Self-consistency decoding and an explicit instruction to ignore irrelevant information partly mitigate this. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00093)
  - Harness note: stale tool output is irrelevant context. Pruning it is a quality fix, not only a cost fix.
- **HELMET: How to Evaluate Long-Context Language Models Effectively and Thoroughly**: Yen, Gao, Hou, Ding et al. (Princeton); arXiv 2410.02694; ICLR 2025. Across 59 long-context models, synthetic NIAH "do[es] not reliably predict downstream performance." Open-source models lag closed ones on full-context reasoning and complex instruction following, and "the gap widens as length increases." RAG-style tasks were the best fast proxy. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02694)
- **LongBench v2**: Bai, Tu, Zhang, Peng et al.; arXiv 2412.15204. 503 hard multiple-choice questions with contexts of 8K–2M words. Human experts score 53.7% under a 15-minute limit, the best model answering directly 50.1%, and o1-preview with longer reasoning 57.7%. Inference-time reasoning helps long-context tasks. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15204)

**2025 studies**
- **NoLiMa: Long-Context Evaluation Beyond Literal Matching**: Modarressi, Deilamsalehy, Dernoncourt, Bui et al. (LMU Munich / Adobe Research; dataset at adobe-research GitHub); arXiv 2502.05167; ICML 2025. The needles have minimal lexical overlap with the question. Of 13 models claiming ≥128K, 11 fall below 50% of their short-context baseline at 32K. GPT-4o drops from 99.3% to 69.7%. Reasoning models and chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting do not fix this. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05167)
  - Harness note: when the agent must connect information semantically rather than lexically (most agent work), the real effective window is far shorter than NIAH suggests. Lexical anchors such as exact file paths, function names and error strings in summaries and notes make later lookups easier.
- **Context Rot: How Increasing Input Tokens Impacts LLM Performance**: Kelly Hong, Anton Troynikov, Jeff Huber (Chroma), technical report, July 14, 2025. 18 models were tested (Claude Opus 4/Sonnet 4/3.7/3.5/Haiku 3.5; o3, GPT-4.1/mini/nano, GPT-4o, GPT-4 Turbo, GPT-3.5 Turbo; Gemini 2.5 Pro/Flash, 2.0 Flash; Qwen3-235B/32B/8B). Findings:
  - Performance degrades faster with length when needle-question similarity is lower.
  - One distractor hurts and four compound the damage. Claude models hallucinate least (Opus 4/Sonnet 4 often abstain), while GPT models hallucinate most.
  - Shuffled (incoherent) haystacks beat coherent ones across all 18 models.
  - On LongMemEval, every model scored significantly better with a focused ~300-token prompt than with the full ~113K-token prompt. Thinking modes narrowed the gap without closing it.
  - On a trivial "repeat these words" task, performance fell consistently with length for all models.
  - Needle *position* caused no notable variation in their NIAH extension.
  - Conclusion: presence of relevant information is not enough, and how it is presented matters. — [Chroma report](https://www.trychroma.com/research/context-rot)
  - Harness note: give the model a focused context, as in the ~300- vs ~113K-token LongMemEval comparison. The Chroma result that position does not matter much on newer models suggests length and distractor management now matter more than position tricks.
- **Context Length Alone Hurts LLM Performance Despite Perfect Retrieval**: Du, Tian, Ronanki, Rongali et al.; arXiv 2510.05381; Findings of EMNLP 2025. Across 5 models on math, QA and coding, performance drops 13.9%–85% as input grows, still within the claimed window, even when retrieval is perfect.
  - The drop persists when irrelevant tokens are replaced with whitespace, when they are attention-masked entirely, and when all evidence is placed immediately before the question.
  - Mitigation: prompting the model to *recite* the retrieved evidence first turns the task back into a short-context one, giving up to +4% for GPT-4o on RULER. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05381)
  - Harness note: this is the strongest evidence that removing tokens beats reorganizing them. Recitation (Manus's todo.md and Anthropic's "quote first") is a cheap, model-agnostic mitigation.
- **LLMs Get Lost In Multi-Turn Conversation**: Laban, Hayashi, Zhou, Neville; arXiv 2505.06120. Across 200K+ simulated conversations, all top models perform worse in multi-turn, underspecified settings than single-turn, with an average drop of 39% across six tasks. The cause is mostly unreliability, not lost aptitude. Models make early assumptions and "when LLMs take a wrong turn in a conversation, they get lost and do not recover." — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06120)
  - Harness note: this supports a "restart with a consolidated spec" action. When a session has drifted, compact into a clean restatement of requirements and start a fresh context rather than appending more corrections.
- **How Many Instructions Can LLMs Follow at Once? (IFScale)**: Jaroslawicz, Whiting, Shah, Maamari (Distyl AI); arXiv 2507.11538. The best frontier models reach only 68% accuracy at 500 simultaneous instructions, with a bias toward earlier instructions. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11538)
  - Harness note: system prompts, AGENTS.md/CLAUDE.md and skills all compete for instruction-following capacity. Keep the always-on instruction set small and load task-specific guidance just in time.
- **Self-Consistency Falls Short! The Adverse Effects of Positional Bias on Long-Context Problems**: Byerly, Khashabi; arXiv 2411.01101. Self-consistency degrades long-context performance. Position bias "worsens with longer context lengths and smaller model sizes." — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01101)
- **LongMemEval**: Wu et al.; arXiv 2410.10813; ICLR 2025. Commercial assistants and long-context LLMs show about a 30% accuracy drop when memorizing information across sustained interactions. (Memory systems are covered by another researcher; this result is cited only for context.) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10813)

**2026 studies (preprints)**
- **Classifier Context Rot: Monitor Performance Degrades with Context Length**: Martin, Roger; arXiv 2605.12366. Used as transcript classifiers, Opus 4.6, GPT 5.4 and Gemini 3.1 miss subtly dangerous coding-agent actions 2×–30× more often when the action follows 800K tokens of benign activity. Periodic reminders throughout the transcript partly mitigate this. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12366)
- **Diagnosing and Mitigating Context Rot in Long-horizon Search**: Xia, Wang, Huang, Liu; arXiv 2606.29718. Under long context, flagship models give up or give uncertain wrong answers ("inconclusive search failures") long before the window is exhausted. Seven context-management methods were analyzed, and all act as test-time scaling by reducing *peak* context length. Behavior-aware filtering of parallel samples gives +2.6% to 4.9%. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29718)
- **When and How Context Rot Appears in Coding Agents (Agent Skills in code auditing)**: Yue Xue; arXiv 2607.17937. Codex with gpt-5.4-mini passes 8/10 runs with a 10,991-character clean context but only 3/10 with a 299,140-character context, whether that context is relevant or irrelevant. This is trend-level, p=0.0698. A detailed external checklist passes 10/10 versus 5/10 for a generic self-check (p=0.0325). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17937)
- **Understanding LLM Performance Degradation in Multi-Instance Processing**: Chen, Pilehvar, Camacho-Collados; arXiv 2603.22608; ACL 2026. When processing many instances (e.g., N reviews), performance degrades slightly at about 20–100 instances and then collapses. Instance count has a stronger effect than context length. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22608)
  - Harness note: for batch-style tool outputs (many search hits, many files, many test failures), cap item counts and fan out to sub-agents instead of feeding hundreds of items at once.

**Vendor guidance on placement**
- Anthropic's prompting docs say to put long documents (20K+ tokens) at the top of the prompt, above the query, instructions and examples: "Queries at the end can improve response quality by up to 30 percent in tests, especially with complex, multidocument inputs." The docs also recommend wrapping documents in `<document>` tags with source metadata and asking the model to quote relevant parts before answering. — [Claude prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices)
- Anthropic engineering describes "context rot" and a finite "attention budget." n tokens give n² pairwise relationships, and models see fewer long sequences in training. Degradation is "gradual, not a hard cliff." — [Anthropic, Effective context engineering for AI agents (Sep 29, 2025)](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)

### Inferences
- **How much context to keep:** use effective length (RULER-style) as the ceiling and aim to operate well under it. For local 7B–14B models in Ollama or llama.cpp, the evidence (RULER effective 16K–64K; NoLiMa collapse by 32K even for frontier models; Du et al. degradation within claimed lengths) suggests a working context of roughly 8K–32K. That is not a reason to use the whole 128K a GGUF advertises.
- **Placement pattern that reconciles caching with recency:** [stable system prompt + tool schemas + project rules] → [compacted history / notes] → [recent raw turns] → [pinned "current goal + todo + constraints" recitation block] → [latest user message or tool result]. The top stays cacheable, and the objective stays in the high-attention tail.
- **Prefer fewer, high-signal tokens over smarter placement.** The 2025–26 evidence (Chroma, Du et al.) shows position effects shrinking but the length penalty remaining. Pruning beats reordering.
- **Lost-in-the-middle has partly been superseded.** The U-curve was strong for 2023 models (GPT-3.5, Claude-1.3). Chroma (2025) found no notable position effect in its NIAH extension, but RULER, NoLiMa and Du et al. show the length penalty is still present in current models.

### Gaps
- I found no rigorous 2026 study that isolates *position* effects on the newest frontier models (Claude 4.6+/5.x, GPT-5.x, Gemini 3.x) in agentic transcripts. Most 2026 work measures length or compaction rather than placement.
- No source quantified how quantization (Q4/Q5 GGUF) changes effective context length for local models. The RULER numbers are for unquantized weights.
- Ollama's docs do not state what happens when a prompt exceeds `num_ctx`/`OLLAMA_CONTEXT_LENGTH` (silent truncation vs. error). See Q6.

## Q2. Context compaction for agents: LLM summarization vs. observation masking, ACON, MemGPT-style paging, recursive and hierarchical summaries, context folding, AgentFold, sliding windows. What works best for long-horizon tool-use agents, and at what cost?

### Takeaway
For coding and tool-use agents, the most robust and cheapest first line is deterministic: mask or clear old tool *observations* beyond a recency window while keeping the agent's own reasoning and actions. It matches LLM summarization on solve rate at about half the cost of a raw agent (JetBrains), and production harnesses ship it (Anthropic context editing, SWE-agent "last 5 observations"). LLM summarization is needed only when masking is not enough. It should be structured, incremental ("anchored") and verified, because 2026 studies show summaries silently drop constraints, file paths and dates, inject unsupported "decisions," and destabilize execution. The best measured combination is rule-based elision first, then LLM summarization (hybrid). Learned approaches (AgentFold, Context-Folding, ACON-distilled, StateComp) do better still but need training. For a third-party-model harness, ACON's prompt-optimized compression guidelines and sub-agent "branch-and-return" are the transferable parts.

### Cited Findings

**Observation masking vs. summarization (core evidence)**
- **The Complexity Trap: Simple Observation Masking Is as Efficient as LLM Summarization for Agent Context Management**: Lindenbauer, Slinko, Felder, Bogomolov et al. (JetBrains Research); arXiv 2508.21433; 4th DL4C workshop at NeurIPS 2025. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.21433); [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.21433v3)
  - Setup: SWE-agent on SWE-bench Verified (n=500), five model configurations, plus a generality probe on OpenHands.
  - Observation masking (OM): rolling window of M=10 turns. Older *observations* are replaced by a placeholder ("Previous 8 lines omitted for brevity"); reasoning and actions are kept in full.
  - LLM-Summary: summarizes N=21 turns at once and keeps the last M=10 raw, so the first summary appears at turn 31.
  - Results (solve rate / instance cost):

    | Model | Raw | OM | LLM-Summary |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | Qwen3-Coder 480B | 53.4% / $1.29 | 54.8% / $0.61 (−52.7%) | 53.8% / $0.64 |
    | Gemini 2.5 Flash | 32.8% / $0.41 | 35.6% / $0.18 (−56.1%) | 36.0% / $0.24 |
    | Qwen3-32B | 17.0% / $1.12 | 15.0% / $0.55 | 16.0% / $0.50 |
    | Gemini 2.5 Flash (thinking) | 40.4% | 36.4% | 31.4% |

    Only the Gemini-thinking drops are statistically significant.
  - Summarization *elongates trajectories*: about 15% longer than Raw and about 13% longer than OM for Qwen3-Coder 480B (Gemini Flash: 52 vs 44 turns). Summary generation was 0.65%–7.2% of instance cost.
  - Hybrid (OM window W=10, then summarization with N=43): +2.6 points over Raw, with cost 7% below OM and 11% below LLM-Summary (Qwen3-Coder 480B, Verified-50). Untuned N=21 made the hybrid less efficient.
  - Harness note: implement OM as the default compaction tier. Keep the last ~10 turns of tool output verbatim, and replace older tool *results* (never the user's messages or the agent's actions) with a one-line placeholder that says what was elided and how to re-fetch it. Use LLM summarization only as a second tier with a large N (summarize big chunks rarely).
- **SWE-agent: Agent-Computer Interfaces Enable Automated Software Engineering**: Yang, Jimenez, Wettig, Lieret et al.; arXiv 2405.15793 (NeurIPS 2024). Ablations on SWE-bench Lite with GPT-4 Turbo (full config 18.0%):
  - Keeping the last 5 observations (older ones collapsed): 18.0%. Full history: 15.0%.
  - File viewer: 100-line window 18.0%, 30 lines 14.3%, full file 12.7%.
  - Search: summarized search 18.0%, iterative search 12.0%, no search 15.7%. Search returns at most 50 results; otherwise the agent is told to refine the query.
  - Edit with lint guardrail 18.0% vs. without 15.0%. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15793); [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15793v3)
  - Harness note: bound observation size at the tool interface (windowed file views, capped search results that refuse with "be more specific") as well as at compaction time.
- **OpenHands context condenser** (blog, April 9, 2025). Older events are summarized once context passes a threshold; recent exchanges stay intact. The summary captures user goals, progress, remaining work, critical files and failing tests. On a SWE-bench Verified subset it solved 54% vs. 53% baseline at up to 2× lower per-turn cost ("less than half" the baseline once condensation starts). Cost growth went from quadratic to linear, with the trade-off of extra turns spent condensing. — [OpenHands blog](https://openhands.dev/blog/openhands-context-condensensation-for-more-efficient-ai-agents)
- **Anthropic context editing + memory tool** (Sep 29, 2025). Context editing removes stale tool calls and results when approaching token limits. On an internal agentic-search eval, memory tool plus context editing gave +39% over baseline, and context editing alone gave +29%. In a 100-turn web-search eval, context editing let agents finish workflows that otherwise failed from context exhaustion while cutting token use by 84%. — [Claude blog: Managing context on the Claude Developer Platform](https://claude.com/blog/context-management)
  - API defaults for `clear_tool_uses_20250919`: `trigger` = 100,000 input tokens; `keep` = 3 most recent tool use/result pairs; optional `clear_at_least` (minimum tokens to clear, so a cache-invalidating edit is worth it); `exclude_tools`; `clear_tool_inputs` default false. Cleared results are replaced with placeholder text. `clear_thinking_20251015` strips old thinking blocks. Client-side SDK compaction defaults to `context_token_threshold: 100,000`; server-side compaction (`compact_20260112`) is recommended as the primary strategy. — [Claude docs: context editing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/context-editing)

**LLM summarization variants and their failure modes**
- **Factory, "Evaluating Context Compression for AI Agents"** (Dec 16, 2025). The study ran probe questions on 36K+ messages from production SWE sessions, judged blind by GPT-5.2 on six 0–5 dimensions. Overall scores: Factory "anchored iterative summarization" (a persistent structured summary with sections for intent, file modifications, decisions and next steps, into which each newly truncated span is *merged*) 3.70; Anthropic SDK compaction (regenerates a 7–12K-character structured summary each time) 3.44; OpenAI `/responses/compact` (opaque) 3.35. Compression ratios were similar (98.6%–99.3%). Artifact tracking scored only 2.19–2.45/5 for all methods ("Artifact tracking remains an unsolved problem"). Generic summaries drop file paths, endpoints and exact error codes. The right target is "tokens per task," not tokens per request. — [Factory](https://factory.com/news/evaluating-compression)
  - Harness note: keep a separate, deterministic artifact ledger (files read, created or modified, commands run, test status) maintained by the harness from tool-call metadata. Do not trust the summarizer for it. Merge summaries incrementally instead of regenerating them.
- **Compact-Bench: Measuring What Context Compaction Costs Long-Horizon Coding Agents**: Sun, Cai, Zhao, Zhou et al.; arXiv 2610.31710 (preprint). Pairs full-history and summary-only continuations from the same repo state (35 SWE-bench repairs, 146 sessions, 106 production compaction chains, 14 summarizer backbones). Stable damage ranges from 5% to 64% of 22 full-history successes. LLM summaries retain at most 16.0% of recurring details, versus 79.1% for mechanical extraction; production summaries retain 15.7% at the first boundary. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31710)
- **Benchmarks Are Surprisingly Compaction-Friendly**: Salle, Bhatnagar, Bethge, Abdelnabi et al.; arXiv 2610.31778 (preprint). In OpenCode with five compaction strategies, three frontier models were run on PaperBench, ProgramBench and PostTrainBench. For the two 1M-context models, the compaction threshold did not measurably matter. GPT-5.3-Codex (smallest window) did worse at lower thresholds. No method beat default self-summarization, and methods that discard more cost some models up to 20 points. The first compaction summary drops about one third of ProgramBench's anti-cheating rules, rising monotonically with more compactions. In real SWE-chat sessions, *how-to-work / don't-do* instructions are dropped about 6× more than specifications of what to build. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31778)
  - Harness note: user-stated constraints and prohibitions must be pinned verbatim outside the summarizable region (a "constraints ledger"). Never route them through an LLM summary.
- **Prune the Summary, Not the History: Source-Judged Compaction**: Min, Chen; arXiv 2610.20927 (preprint). Compaction summaries mix environment-established facts with compressor-written reasoning ("decisions/next steps"). In 83% of failed runs, the failure traces back to an action that carries out a summary entry, mostly one with no environment support. A cheap per-entry "is this sourced?" judge that deletes unsupported entries improves cross-run reliability by 2.1–8.9% on AppWorld/OfficeBench without adding agent tokens. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20927)
- **Towards Reliable Context Compression for Long-Horizon Agents: Execution Instability (TRACE)**: Min, Wu, Darbari, Chen et al.; arXiv 2608.06503; and **PAIR (counterfactual continuations)**: Min, Wu, Shi, He et al.; arXiv 2609.36526 (preprints). Compression weakens the influence of recent interactions, increasing blocked actions, repeated exploration and cross-run instability. It "degrades reliability before solvability," and severe damage concentrates at isolated compression events. Optimizing the compression *prompt* with paired continuations brings compressed execution close to no-compression. — [arXiv 2608.06503](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06503); [arXiv 2609.36526](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36526)
- **What Does Context Compression Cost an Agent?**: Shuyu Liu; arXiv 2608.16370 (preprint). Compression can leave task completion unchanged while forcing the agent to re-acquire dropped state. For GPT-5.5, completion went from 80% to 85% (n.s.) while retrieval calls rose from 21.0 to 63.9 (p=.002). The effect is environment-dependent; there was no surge in ALFWorld. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16370)
- **The Sleeping Agent: What Gist-Based Context Compression Loses and Why**: Kyrkewood; arXiv 2608.11775 (preprint). Gist summaries beat truncation on multi-hop and factual questions but discard dates and times. A one-sentence prompt change raised temporal-expression preservation from 3.05% to 62.39% and recovered +0.314 judge accuracy on temporal questions. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11775)
- **Recursively Summarizing Enables Long-Term Dialogue Memory in LLMs**: Wang, Fu, Cao, Wang et al.; arXiv 2308.15022; Neurocomputing. Each new memory is produced from the previous memory plus new context, which improves consistency and complements 8K/16K long-context and retrieval-augmented models. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.15022)
  - Harness note: this is the origin of "summary of (old summary + new span)." Factory, ACE (Q4) and the Compact-Bench and constraint-loss results argue for *structured incremental merges* over free-form recursive rewriting, which loses details at each pass.

**Optimized / learned compression usable with closed models**
- **ACON: Optimizing Context Compression for Long-horizon LLM Agents**: Kang, Chen, Han, Inan et al. (Microsoft; code at github.com/microsoft/acon); arXiv 2510.00615; ICML 2026. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.00615); [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.00615v3)
  - Method: compresses both history and individual observations. It optimizes the *natural-language compression guideline* by collecting contrastive tasks (full context succeeds, compressed fails). An optimizer LLM (o3) diagnoses what was lost and updates the guideline (utility step), then shortens it using successful compressed runs (compression step). The compressor can then be distilled to small models.
  - Thresholds: history compression above 4,096 tokens and observation compression above 1,024 tokens ("moderate values … provide the best trade-off").
  - AppWorld (gpt-4.1): no compression 56.0% at 9.93K peak tokens; FIFO 45.8%; retrieval 27.4%; LLMLingua 39.3%; naive prompting 43.5%; ACON ut+co 56.5% at 7.33K peak. Overall, peak tokens fall 26–54%.
  - Distilled Qwen3-14B/8B/Phi-4 compressors keep over 95% of teacher performance at $0.0004 vs $0.045 per example. ACON lifts Qwen3-14B *as agent* from 25.6% to 33.9% on AppWorld ("up to 46%" improvement for small LMs).
  - Harness note: this transfers directly to Xylo. Ship a compression-guideline prompt, then tune it offline on Xylo's own failure pairs (a full-context success paired with a compressed failure). Run the compressor on a cheap local model (Qwen3-8B/14B via Ollama). Use ACON's thresholds (observation >1K, history >4K) as starting points for small-window local models.
- **AgentDiet / Reducing Cost of LLM Agents with Trajectory Reduction**: Xiao, Gao, Peng, Xiong; arXiv 2509.23586; FSE 2026. Inference-time removal of "useless, redundant, and expired" trajectory content in a top coding agent cuts input tokens by 39.9%–59.7% and total cost by 21.1%–35.9% at the same performance (two LLMs, two benchmarks). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23586)
- **FOCUS: Training-Free Decision-Preserving Context Compression for LLM Agents**: Dixit, Bastos, Zhang, Bansal et al.; arXiv 2609.37590 (preprint). Test-time causal "decision preservation" over interaction units works with closed-API models and cuts peak context by up to 48% while improving success by up to 8.9 points over uncompressed. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37590)
- **When Can Agents Forget Their Reasoning? (ICLR, Interaction-Aware Compression)**: Wang, Luo, Wang, Yao et al.; arXiv 2609.29875 (preprint). Pruning *historical reasoning blocks* (ranked by a frozen proxy model's entropy) while preserving actions, tool calls and observations raised reward from 0.699 to 0.718 on 260 tasks. Input, output and cache-read tokens fell 25.5%, 14.4% and 33.3%. Reasoning becomes replaceable once derived state has been externalized to code, files or tool outputs. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29875)
  - Note the opposite choice to JetBrains OM, which drops *observations* and keeps reasoning. The two are complementary.
- **StateComp: Learning When to Compress History**: Wang, Chen, Guo, Luo et al.; arXiv 2609.27298 (preprint). A router trained on frozen-LM hidden states decides when past interactions are "READY" to compress, cutting agent plus summarization tokens by 52.27% at equal task performance. It needs hidden states, so it suits local models only. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27298)
- **Continuous Context Management (CCM)**: Hoy, Fan, Celik, Pan; arXiv 2609.35540 (preprint). Compacting on *every* turn (prompt = task + rolling memory + newest observation) without fine-tuning lowered task success for most models on TerminalBench-2 (Sonnet 4.6, Opus 4.6, GLM-5); only Kimi K3 kept performance. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35540)
  - Harness note: do not compact every turn with off-the-shelf models. Threshold-triggered, batched compaction is safer.

**Hierarchical, folding and branch-and-return**
- **HiAgent: Hierarchical Working Memory Management**: Hu, Chen, Chen, Mu et al.; arXiv 2408.09559. The agent formulates subgoals and replaces each completed subgoal's action-observation pairs with a summary. Across five long-horizon tasks this gives 2× success rate and 3.8 fewer steps on average. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.09559)
- **THREAD: Thinking Deeper with Recursive Spawning**: Schroeder, Morgan, Luo, Glass; arXiv 2405.17402. Threads spawn child threads that return only the tokens the parent needs. It is state of the art with GPT-4/3.5 on ALFWorld, TextCraft and WebShop, and gains 10–50 absolute points over prior frameworks with small models (Llama-3-8B, CodeLlama-7B). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17402)
  - Harness note: branch-and-return is especially valuable for small local models.
- **Scaling Long-Horizon LLM Agent via Context-Folding (FoldGRPO)**: Sun, Lu, Ling, Liu et al.; arXiv 2510.11967. The agent "branch[es] into a sub-trajectory to handle a subtask and then fold[s] it upon completion," keeping only a concise outcome summary. RL-trained with process rewards, it matches or beats ReAct on Deep Research and SWE tasks with a 10× smaller active context and "significantly outperforms" summarization-based context management. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11967)
- **AgentFold: Long-Horizon Web Agents with Proactive Context Management**: Ye, Zhang, Li, Yin et al. (Alibaba Tongyi Lab); arXiv 2510.24699. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.24699); [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.24699v1)
  - Context = (question, tools, multi-scale state summaries, latest full interaction).
  - Each step emits a fold directive `{"range":[k,t-1],"summary":…}`: either a granular condensation of the last step or a deep consolidation that merges a range of prior summaries.
  - Context grows from about 3.5K to about 7K tokens over 100 turns, 92% (over 84K tokens) smaller than ReAct at turn 100. It stays mostly under 20K tokens at 500 turns.
  - AgentFold-30B-A3B (supervised fine-tuning only) scores 36.2% on BrowseComp, 47.3% on BrowseComp-ZH, 62.1% on WideSearch and 67.0% on GAIA, beating o4-mini (28.3) on BrowseComp.
  - Harness note: the fold-directive *format* can be emulated with prompting. Ask the model to output an optional `fold(range, summary)` alongside each action and apply it in the harness. Gains were shown only for a fine-tuned model.
- **ReSum: Unlocking Long-Horizon Search Intelligence via Context Summarization**: Wu, Li, Zhao, Zhang et al.; arXiv 2509.13313. A plug-and-play summary tool periodically condenses history, giving +4.5% over ReAct training-free and a further +8.2% with ReSum-GRPO. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.13313)
- **SUPO (Summarization-augmented Policy Optimization)**: Lu, Sun, Du, Ling et al.; arXiv 2510.06727. RL training with summarization-based context management improves success at the same or lower working context, and lets agents scale summary rounds beyond those seen in training. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06727)
- **Anthropic multi-agent research system** (Jun 13, 2025). Sub-agents use separate contexts and return condensed findings. An Opus 4 lead with Sonnet 4 sub-agents beat single-agent Opus 4 by 90.2% on an internal research eval. Token usage alone explained 80% of BrowseComp variance (95% with tool-call count and model choice). Multi-agent systems use about 15× the tokens of chat (single agents about 4×). The lead saves its plan to memory because past 200,000 tokens the context "will be truncated." — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)
  - The effective context engineering post says sub-agents may use "tens of thousands of tokens or more" but return "often 1,000–2,000 tokens." — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)

**Paging / recoverable (lossless-ish) approaches**
- **MemGPT: Towards LLMs as Operating Systems**: Packer, Wooders, Lin, Fang et al.; arXiv 2310.08560. OS-style "virtual context management" pages data between in-context (fast) and external (slow) tiers using interrupts and function calls. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08560) (Memory-store aspects are covered by another researcher.)
- **DTOC: Dynamic Tool Output Compression**: Chaturvedi, Bhattacharya, Gopalkrishnan, van der Putten; arXiv 2609.26121; Discovery Science 2026. Full tool outputs go to external memory with compact placeholders in context and on-demand restore. On DeepSWE with Sonnet 4.6 and GPT-5.4: input −10.3% and −12.7%, solve rates 2.5× and 1.5× higher, cost per solved task 3× and 3.5× lower. Results are mixed for other models. Ablation: "reversibility is critical," and disable-only (non-restorable) compression degraded performance. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26121)
- **An Empirical Study of Harness Design for Coding Agents**: Fan, Zhang, Ma, Hu et al.; arXiv 2609.20804 (preprint). 176 matched settings across 4 models on SWE-bench Verified and Terminal-Bench 2.1, with five context-management strategies and four window budgets.
  - Context management becomes more valuable as the budget tightens, mostly by preventing overflow failures.
  - "Staging rule-based elision before LLM-based summarization provides the strongest overall efficiency."
  - Making elided content recoverable "adds machinery that models rarely use and yields no accuracy gain."
  - Context management lengthens trajectories without much change in agent behavior. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20804)
  - **Conflict:** DTOC (above) reports that reversibility is critical, while this study finds recoverability unused and without gain. Cursor and Manus (Q4) also advocate restorable compression. The difference may come from the strategy design or the models, so treat recoverability as cheap insurance rather than a proven accuracy lever.
- **Agent-Controlled Forgetting: Reversible Context Curation in Practice**: Franke; arXiv 2610.10590 (preprint, exploratory). The model itself archives selected tool results, leaving notes in place and keeping originals recoverable. In one debugging case it used 231,951 prompt tokens vs. 912,492 (about 50% fewer cumulative input tokens; about $1.28–1.44 vs. $4.38), but 17% longer wall time. Another workload saw no savings. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10590)
- **Memex(RL)**: Wang, Chen, Wang, Wei; arXiv 2603.04257. The working context holds structured summaries plus stable *indices*, and full-fidelity interactions live in an external store the agent can dereference. RL-trained, it improves success with a smaller working context. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04257)
- **ACM: Agentic Context Management for Long Horizon Tasks**: Li, Ming, Chu, Shao et al.; arXiv 2607.23809. Context-editing tools let the agent decide when to compress, offload discarded content to external memory and query it later. It comes with a post-training pipeline and reduces peak token pressure and cross-trial variance. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23809)
- **Recursive Language Models (RLMs)**: Zhang, Kraska, Khattab; arXiv 2512.24601. The long prompt is treated as part of an external environment (a REPL variable) that the LLM programmatically examines, decomposes and recursively sub-calls over. It handles inputs two orders of magnitude beyond the window. On GPT-5, median gains across four long-context tasks were +26% vs. compaction, +130% vs. CodeAct with sub-calls and +13% vs. Claude Code, at comparable cost. RLM-Qwen3-8B improves on Qwen3-8B by 28.3%. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24601)
- **Context as an Environment (Scroll)**: Lin, Ang, Zhu, Ding et al.; arXiv 2608.21690 (preprint). Each session is an append-only event log plus a persistent Python kernel. Tool outputs are bound to variables, and only printed projections enter the prompt. Evicted spans keep "landmarks" pointing to exact log addresses. With Qwen3.8-Max it reports 94.8% on LongMemEval_S and 86.7% on LOCA_256K. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21690)

**Small-window / local-model evidence**
- **Bud ContextCompact 2.0**: VG, PS; arXiv 2610.25516 (preprint). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25516)
  - Recall over recorded sessions: compaction to a 16K budget raised a 1.2B model to 0.335, versus 0.247 with truncation and 0.176 with its full 32K history. A 9B model went from 0.515 (truncation) to 0.577.
  - 100 SWE-bench Verified tasks with a 27B reasoning model in a 49K window: compaction raised the resolve rate on *long* sessions from 41% to 53% (+12.3 points, p=0.043) with 13% fewer tokens per solved task. Short sessions were unchanged.
  - The main failure without compaction was "a reasoning turn exhausting the room the history left," not overflow. Gains appeared only when compaction triggered early enough.
  - Each summary took a median of 161 s locally, and the gain vanished under a 45-minute session budget.
- **LOHA / Anchored Context Distillation**: Zou, Wang, Hao; arXiv 2609.31430 (preprint; requires training). Older observations become soft tokens while the last K observations stay in text. Under a 32K limit, Qwen3-4B with K=3 resolved 21.1% vs. 11.1% for full text on a 199-instance subset. Without the limit it was slightly worse than the uncompressed base (12.1% vs 14.5%). Larger K generally favored accuracy. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31430)

**Cost/latency side effects of compaction**
- **Beyond Token Savings: A Systematic Study of Context Compression in LLM Agents**: Satish, Sinha, Kawada, Yadwadkar; arXiv 2609.32961 (preprint). Nearly 35,000 runs with three open-weight models on SWE-bench Verified and Terminal-Bench. On Terminal-Bench with Qwen, policies using about one third the tokens took 20–80% longer. Policies with similar success solve different tasks, and the same policy behaves differently across models. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32961)

### Inferences
- **Recommended compaction ladder for Xylo**, cheapest and safest first:
  1. **Bound at the source.** Use windowed reads, capped search and head/tail of long outputs, and spill full output to a file with a pointer (SWE-agent, Cursor, Manus).
  2. **Observation masking.** Replace tool results older than the last K turns (K≈3 per Anthropic's default, up to 10 per JetBrains) with a placeholder naming the tool, arguments, a size and a re-fetch hint. Never mask user messages, the agent's actions, or pinned constraints. Apply it in *batches* with a minimum clear size (Anthropic `clear_at_least`) to limit cache busts (see Q5).
  3. **Structured LLM summarization** of the masked middle, triggered rarely (large N). Use a fixed template: intent; user constraints *verbatim*; decisions *with evidence*; files touched (from the harness ledger, not the LLM); errors seen; next steps. Merge incrementally rather than regenerating. Optionally run a cheap "is this entry sourced?" check (SJC), and explicitly require dates, IDs, paths and error codes (Sleeping Agent).
  4. **Branch-and-return sub-agents** for exploratory subtasks (search, log triage, reading many files) that return 1–2K-token reports (Anthropic, THREAD, Context-Folding).
  5. **Fresh-context restart** from on-disk state (progress file, todo, git log) when the session has drifted (Laban; Anthropic docs, Q4).
- **Cost to expect:** LLM summaries cost about 1–7% of instance cost directly, but can lengthen trajectories by about 13–15% (JetBrains), add wall-clock time (161 s per summary locally; 20–80% longer runs in Beyond Token Savings), and trigger re-acquisition tool calls (up to 3× more retrieval calls).
- **Learned methods** (AgentFold, FoldGRPO, SUPO, ReSum-GRPO, StateComp, MemexRL) show the ceiling but need fine-tuned or open models. For a third-party-model harness, the usable parts are their *context layouts*: multi-scale summaries plus the latest full interaction, and branch/return.

### Gaps
- I could not open the Context-Folding full text (the arXiv HTML returned 404), so its exact tools, budgets (e.g., the active-context limit) and benchmark numbers beyond "10× smaller active context" are unverified.
- No head-to-head of prompted (untrained) AgentFold-style folding vs. JetBrains-style masking on frontier closed models was found.
- The JetBrains results come from SWE-agent and SWE-bench. Evidence for non-coding general agents (desktop, email, browsing) is thinner: Anthropic's internal evals, ACON on AppWorld/OfficeBench, and web-search studies.
- Conflicting evidence on reversibility (DTOC vs. the harness design study) is unresolved.

## Q3. Prompt/context compression methods (LLMLingua family, Selective Context, RECOMP, gist tokens): which are usable at the harness level vs. which need model access? Which strategies are specific to tool output?

### Takeaway
Token-dropping compressors (LLMLingua, LongLLMLingua, LLMLingua-2, Selective Context) and trained extractive or abstractive compressors (RECOMP, Paritok-4B, distilled ACON) run *beside* the target model on a small local model, so a harness can use them with any API. Gist tokens, soft-token observation compression (LOHA/ACD), attention-guided trajectory pruning (AttnCompress) and KV-level methods need weights or internals, so they apply only to Xylo's local llama.cpp/Ollama path, if at all. For agents, 2026 evidence warns that (a) prose compressors mangle code identifiers, (b) query-aware compression breaks prompt caching, and (c) tool-output token savings often do *not* reduce billed cost. The best tool-output strategy is to bound and spill to files at the source, use extractive (not paraphrasing) compression, and use retention policies that vary by tool.

### Cited Findings

**General-purpose prompt compressors (harness-usable with a small local LM)**
- **LLMLingua**: Jiang, Wu, Lin, Yang et al. (Microsoft); arXiv 2310.05736; EMNLP 2023. Coarse-to-fine token dropping uses a small LM's perplexity with a budget controller and iterative token-level compression. Up to 20× compression "with little performance loss" on GSM8K, BBH, ShareGPT and arXiv. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05736)
- **LongLLMLingua**: Jiang, Wu, Luo, Li et al. (Microsoft); arXiv 2310.06839; ACL 2024. *Question-aware* compression plus reordering against position bias. On NaturalQuestions it gives up to +21.4% with about 4× fewer tokens (GPT-3.5-Turbo). It cut cost 94.0% on LooGLE and sped end-to-end latency 1.4–2.6× at 2–6× compression of ~10K-token prompts. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06839)
- **LLMLingua-2**: Pan, Wu, Jiang, Xia et al. (Microsoft); arXiv 2403.12968; Findings of ACL 2024. Compression is framed as token *classification* with a bidirectional encoder (XLM-RoBERTa-large / mBERT) distilled from GPT-4, which keeps the output faithful (extractive). It runs 3–6× faster than prior compressors, with 1.6–2.9× end-to-end speedup at 2–5× compression, and generalizes across target LLMs. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12968)
  - Harness note: LLMLingua-2 is the most practical for a Rust desktop app. It is a small encoder that can run on CPU (via ONNX) and is task-agnostic, so the compressed text can be cached. Apply it to *prose-like* tool outputs (web pages, docs), not to code.
- **Selective Context**: Li, Dong, Lin, Guerin; arXiv 2310.06201; EMNLP 2023. Prunes low self-information lexical units using a small causal LM. 50% context reduction gives 36% less inference memory and 32% less time, with only −0.023 BERTScore and −0.038 faithfulness. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06201)
- **RECOMP**: Xu, Shi, Choi; arXiv 2310.04408. Trained extractive and abstractive compressors for retrieved documents compress to as little as 6% with minimal loss, can return an empty string when a document is irrelevant ("selective augmentation"), and transfer across LMs. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04408)
  - Harness note: the "return empty if irrelevant" behavior is a useful pattern for web or search tool results.

**Methods requiring model training or internals (local-model path only)**
- **Gist tokens (Learning to Compress Prompts with Gist Tokens)**: Mu, Li, Goodman; arXiv 2304.08467; NeurIPS 2023. The LM is trained, via attention-mask changes, to compress prompts into cached "gist" tokens: up to 26× compression and up to 40% fewer FLOPs. This requires fine-tuning the target model, so it is not usable with third-party APIs. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08467)
- **LOHA + Anchored Context Distillation**: arXiv 2609.31430. Soft-token compression of old observations requires training the agent model. See Q2 for numbers. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31430)
- **AttnCompress: Dynamic Attention-Guided Trajectory Compression for SWE Agents**: Zeng, Li, Xie et al.; arXiv 2609.08318 (from title and metadata only; uses attention signals, so it needs model internals). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08318)
- **StreamingLLM (attention sinks)**: Xiao, Tian, Chen, Han et al.; arXiv 2309.17453; ICLR 2024. A pure sliding KV window fails once text exceeds the cache, but keeping the first tokens' KV ("attention sinks") restores stable generation to 4M+ tokens, up to 22.2× faster than sliding-window recomputation. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17453)
  - Harness note: this is the principle behind llama.cpp's `--keep N` and context shift. If Xylo ever relies on context shift for local models, keep the system prompt as the protected prefix. Semantic compaction is still preferable because shifting silently drops middle content.

**Agent and tool-output-specific compression (2025–2026)**
- **Paritok-4B: Intent-Conditioned Context Compression for Coding Agents**: Shi, Chen; arXiv 2608.24188 (preprint). General prose compressors "paraphrase identifiers and drop the exact spans an agent needs to edit." Paritok is an *extractive*, intent-conditioned 4B LoRA (Qwen3-4B) distilled from 67K OpenHands trajectories.
  - 96.0% of the identifiers, paths and numbers it emits already appear in its input.
  - It compresses to 25.7% of original size while keeping 86.5% of uncompressed single-shot SWE-bench Lite quality.
  - It runs on one 24 GB GPU. Using gpt-5 as a compressor is "net-negative," costing more than the tokens it saves. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24188)
- **An Empirical Cost Attribution of Context-Compression Gateways in Multi-Turn Coding Agents**: Chen, Shi; arXiv 2609.22114 (preprint; same team as Paritok). Instrumented Claude Code and Codex with Sonnet and GPT-5.
  - Tool-schema filtering removes about 21K–57K tokens on a typical turn and is "the only unambiguously and reproducibly positive lever."
  - Content compression of file reads saves only about 2% of the cache-priced prefix per turn, but grows roughly quadratically (about 3,350·N² tokens) because compressed reads persist in history. It overtakes the schema saving after about 6 turns.
  - Recall of originals costs a fixed amount per segment.
  - Single-shot compression benchmarks "must not be cited as a cost-saving argument" for multi-turn agents. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22114)
- **Token Reduction Is Not Cost Reduction**: Weinberger, Hozez; arXiv 2607.12161 (preprint). Against a Claude Code baseline, the most aggressive tool-output compression cut delivered tool-output tokens by 38.4% but *raised* billed cost by 6.8%. Token reduction correlated only weakly with cost reduction (r=0.15). Prompt-cache creation and reads dominate input-side cost, and compression changed trajectories (more retrieval and turns). On a SWE-bench Go subset, aggressive compression reduced successful patch application. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12161)
- **TRACER: Per-Tool Context Retention via Consequence-Attributed RL**: Lin, Wu, Yang, Liu et al.; arXiv 2608.29363 (preprint). A query-conditioned retention ratio *per tool output*, accounting for re-invocation costs, cuts total tokens 29–46% versus keeping everything, and 15–18% more than a static per-tool-type policy. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29363)
  - Harness note: even without RL, a static per-tool retention table (e.g., keep `edit` diffs and test-failure summaries longer; mask `ls`, `grep`, page fetches sooner) is a cheap approximation that TRACER beats by only 15–18%.
- **Measure Before You Manage: Evaluating Agent Working Memory in Coding Agents**: Chen, Wan, Sun, Ma et al.; arXiv 2608.31057 (preprint). Across 55 trajectories, instructions, artifacts, tool outputs and agent state show distinct retention and compression behavior. Object-aware policies' calibration gains may not transfer to held-out tasks, and "equal token budgets do not imply equal delivered context or management cost." — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31057)
- **Local-Splitter**: Agyemang, Kponyo, Amponsah, Boakye et al.; arXiv 2604.12301 (preprint). A local Ollama model acts as a triage layer in front of a cloud model, behind an MCP and OpenAI-compatible shim. Local routing plus prompt compression saves 45–79% of cloud tokens on edit-heavy and explanation-heavy workloads. On RAG-heavy work, the full tactic set including local-draft/cloud-review saves 51%. The optimal subset is workload-dependent. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12301)
  - Harness note: this maps directly onto Xylo's hybrid local and cloud setup. A local model can pre-compress or triage tool output before it reaches a cloud model.
- **TurnCost: Turns, Not Observations**: Kumar, Pandey; arXiv 2610.15093 (preprint). In 600 public coding trajectories, median observations are only 118–286 tokens, and the large build logs usually cited to motivate compression sit near the 99th percentile. Turn count explains 84% of the variance in log trajectory cost, versus 4% for mean observation size. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15093)
- **Same Simulation, Different Evidence: Where Retrieved Tool Output Costs LLM Agents Accuracy**: Zhang, Chundur, Owusu, Qomi et al.; arXiv 2610.26836 (preprint). With a geochemistry simulator whose reports reach 846.8K characters, reading through a section index instead of in context cost GPT-5.1 and GPT-5.2 11.3 and 7.9 points of accuracy. Claude Opus 4.6 and Sonnet 4.6 showed no detectable cost. The failure is "evidence substitution": the model answers from the wrong file. Naming the authoritative output raised accuracy 6.3–7.9 points, and inlining outputs that fit worked as well. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26836)
  - Harness note: when spilling tool output to files, the placeholder should say which file is authoritative and how to read the relevant part. For models that fit it, inline small outputs rather than spilling them.

**Tool-output practices from industry**
- **Cursor, "Dynamic context discovery"** (Jan 6, 2026). Cursor writes long shell and MCP outputs to files instead of truncating, and the agent uses `tail` and reads more as needed, which gave "fewer unnecessary summarizations." After summarization, chat history is kept as a searchable file. MCP tool descriptions are synced to folders with only names loaded up front, which "reduced total agent tokens by 46.9%" in runs that called an MCP tool (A/B test, high variance). Terminal output is synced to files for grep. — [Cursor blog](https://cursor.com/blog/dynamic-context-discovery)
- **Manus**: "restorable compression." Drop a web page's content but keep its URL; drop a document's content but keep its sandbox path. — [Manus blog](https://manus.im/blog/Context-Engineering-for-AI-Agents-Lessons-from-Building-Manus)
- **Anthropic**: just-in-time retrieval keeps lightweight identifiers (paths, queries, links) and loads data at runtime. Claude Code uses `head`/`tail` on large data. Tool-result clearing is "one of the lightest forms of compaction." — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)

### Inferences
- **Harness-level (no target-model internals):** LLMLingua, LongLLMLingua, LLMLingua-2, Selective Context (a small local LM or encoder), RECOMP-style trained compressors, Paritok-style extractive local compressors, ACON-optimized prompts with a distilled compressor, AgentDiet-style trajectory pruning, rule-based truncation and spill-to-file.
- **Model access needed:** gist tokens, LOHA/ACD soft tokens, AttnCompress, StateComp (hidden states), KVMem (Q5), attention-sink and KV-eviction methods. For Xylo these are relevant only to local inference, and only with control over llama.cpp internals.
- **For code-centric tool output, prefer extractive selection of whole lines** over token-level dropping or paraphrase (Paritok). Never compress the region the agent is about to edit.
- **Use query-agnostic compression for anything in the cached prefix.** Query-aware compression such as LongLLMLingua produces a different prefix each call and destroys cache reuse (see CAPC in Q5).
- **The biggest cost lever is the fixed preamble** (system prompt plus tool schemas) multiplied by turn count, not observation size. Tool-schema filtering and deferral (Cursor's MCP folders, at 46.9%) beat observation compression on cost (gateway study; What Does a Harness Buy, Q4). Tool selection itself is covered by another researcher.

### Gaps
- No head-to-head evaluation of LLMLingua-2 on *agent tool outputs* (shell logs, JSON, HTML) in a multi-turn agent was found. Its evidence is on QA, summarization and meeting benchmarks.
- Many 2026 tool-output papers are single-team preprints (Paritok and the gateway study share authors) and have not been replicated.

## Q4. Context engineering as a discipline: ACE, Dynamic Cheatsheet, Anthropic's guidance, Manus's lessons, context rot, and industry posts with measured results

### Takeaway
"Context engineering" has converged on a small set of principles. Treat context as a finite attention budget. Keep a stable, cache-friendly prefix with append-only history. Retrieve just in time with lightweight identifiers. Externalize state to the file system (todo or progress files, notes, artifacts). Recite goals near the end. Keep errors visible. Isolate exploratory work in sub-agents. Evolve reusable "playbooks" through incremental, itemized edits rather than wholesale rewrites, which collapse context. The measured results in this area (Anthropic +29–39% and −84% tokens; Cursor −46.9% tokens; ACE +10.6%; OpenHands 2× cheaper) mostly come from vendors' own evals and should be read as directional.

### Cited Findings
- **Anthropic, "Effective context engineering for AI agents"** (Sep 29, 2025; Rajasekaran, Dixon, Ryan, Hadfield). Context engineering means curating "the optimal set of tokens (information) during LLM inference." — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)
  - System prompts at the "right altitude": the minimal set of information that fully defines behavior. Start minimal and add instructions based on observed failures. Organize sections with XML or Markdown.
  - Tools should be token-efficient with minimal overlap. Use a few canonical examples rather than lists of edge cases.
  - Just-in-time retrieval with a hybrid approach: Claude Code loads CLAUDE.md up front and uses glob and grep at runtime.
  - Long-horizon techniques: (1) compaction, where Claude Code keeps architectural decisions, unresolved bugs and implementation details, drops redundant tool outputs, and continues with the summary plus the 5 most recently accessed files. Tune for recall first, then precision. (2) Structured note-taking, such as NOTES.md and to-do lists (the Pokémon agent tracked progress over thousands of steps). (3) Sub-agents returning 1–2K-token summaries.
- **Anthropic context editing and memory tool** (Sep 29, 2025): +29% (editing), +39% (editing plus memory) on an internal agentic-search eval; −84% tokens on a 100-turn web-search eval. — [Claude blog](https://claude.com/blog/context-management)
- **Anthropic prompting docs on multi-window work.** "Context awareness" in Sonnet 4.5+/Haiku 4.5 lets the model track its remaining token budget. For tasks spanning windows: set up a framework in the first window, keep tests in structured files (tests.json), keep progress files and git logs, and create init scripts. The docs say to "consider starting with a brand new context window rather than using compaction," because the latest models are "extremely effective at discovering state from the local filesystem." — [Claude docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices)
- **Manus, "Context Engineering for AI Agents: Lessons from Building Manus"** (Yichao "Peak" Ji, Jul 18, 2025). — [Manus](https://manus.im/blog/Context-Engineering-for-AI-Agents-Lessons-from-Building-Manus)
  - KV-cache hit rate is "the single most important metric." The average input:output token ratio is about 100:1, and Claude Sonnet cached input costs $0.30/MTok vs. $3/MTok uncached (10×).
  - Keep the prefix stable (no second-precision timestamps at the top), keep context append-only, serialize deterministically (JSON key order), and insert cache breakpoints manually (at least at the end of the system prompt). For self-hosted vLLM, enable prefix caching and route by session ID.
  - Mask tools instead of removing them. Tool definitions sit near the front, so changes invalidate the cache and references to missing tools confuse the model. Manus uses a state machine with logit masking or prefill modes (Auto, Required, Specified) and consistent tool-name prefixes (`browser_`, `shell_`) to restrict groups.
  - Use the file system as unlimited, persistent, agent-operable context, with restorable compression.
  - Recite goals via todo.md. A typical task has about 50 tool calls, and rewriting the todo keeps the plan in recent attention against lost-in-the-middle drift.
  - Keep errors and stack traces in context so the model updates its beliefs.
  - Avoid few-shot ruts: repetitive action/observation pairs cause imitation (drift in a 20-resume batch). Add controlled variation to serialization.
- **Cursor, "Dynamic context discovery"** (Jan 6, 2026). Files are the context interface: long outputs, chat history after summarization, skills, MCP tool descriptions (−46.9% tokens) and terminal sessions. — [Cursor](https://cursor.com/blog/dynamic-context-discovery)
- **Factory compression evaluation** (Dec 16, 2025). Anchored incremental summaries beat regenerated ones, artifact tracking is unsolved, and the metric to optimize is "tokens per task." — [Factory](https://factory.com/news/evaluating-compression)
- **OpenHands condenser** (Apr 9, 2025): 54% vs 53% solve rate at under half the per-turn cost, with linear rather than quadratic cost growth. — [OpenHands](https://openhands.dev/blog/openhands-context-condensensation-for-more-efficient-ai-agents)
- **ACE: Agentic Context Engineering: Evolving Contexts for Self-Improving Language Models**: Zhang, Hu, Upasani, Ma et al.; arXiv 2510.04618; ICLR 2026. ACE names two failure modes of iterative context rewriting: *brevity bias* (insights dropped for concise summaries) and *context collapse* (iterative rewriting erodes detail). ACE treats context as an evolving "playbook" maintained by generator, reflector and curator roles, using *structured, incremental* updates. Results: +10.6% on agents and +8.6% on finance. It works offline (system prompts) and online (agent memory), learns from execution feedback without labels, lowers adaptation latency and rollout cost, and matches the top AppWorld production agent overall with a smaller open model (beating it on test-challenge). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04618)
  - Harness note: apply ACE's "delta, not rewrite" rule to every LLM-maintained artifact in Xylo: compaction summaries, project notes, a learned "playbook" of repo conventions. Store each as itemized bullets with IDs and let the LLM add, edit or delete items rather than regenerate the whole document.
- **Dynamic Cheatsheet: Test-Time Learning with Adaptive Memory**: Suzgun, Yuksekgonul, Bianchi, Jurafsky et al. (Stanford); arXiv 2504.07952. A black-box LM keeps a self-curated, persistent cheatsheet of reusable strategies and code snippets, concise rather than whole transcripts. Claude 3.5 Sonnet's AIME accuracy more than doubled. GPT-4o went from 10% to 99% on Game of 24. Equation balancing reached near-perfect from about 50%. GPQA-Diamond improved 9% and MMLU-Pro 8% (Claude). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07952) (Cross-session memory is covered by another researcher. The relevance here is the curated, compact in-context artifact.)
- **Chroma, Context Rot** (Jul 14, 2025): "How information is presented matters more," so context should be carefully constructed. — [Chroma](https://www.trychroma.com/research/context-rot)
- **A Survey of Context Engineering for Large Language Models**: Mei, Yao, Ge, Wang et al.; arXiv 2507.13334. A taxonomy (retrieval and generation, processing, management, and system integrations such as RAG, memory, tools and multi-agent) built from over 1,400 papers. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.13334)
- **What Does a Harness Buy? Tokens, Mostly**: Liu, Han; arXiv 2610.04433 (preprint). Five models were run through Claude Code, mini-SWE-agent and OpenCode on SWE-bench Verified. Pass rates were equivalent within noise (swapping harness flips 13% of tasks, the same as a rerun). Cost per task differed by up to 3×, set "at the first call, by the preamble of system prompt and tool schemas … and scaled by the number of steps." One harness lost points to output caps cutting runs short. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04433)
- **ContextEvo: Self-Evolving Context Management Policies for Long-Horizon Agent Harnesses**: Li, Xu, Chen, Wang et al.; arXiv 2609.34649 (preprint). Context policy is learned from trajectories by reconstructing model-visible context at decision points and fixing context-related failures. Starting from the Pi-agent harness, results were comparable to or better than Codex, OpenCode and OpenClaw on three long-horizon benchmarks. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34649)
- **ContextPipe: Database-Inspired Context Assembly**: Xu, Zhang, Sun, Tian et al.; arXiv 2609.00749 (preprint). Context assembly is treated like query planning (Plan → Bind → Optimize → Execute → Feedback) with a cache-aware optimizer and an EXPLAIN-style trace. Versus append-only on a SWE-bench Pro subset: 31% fewer tokens, 23% fewer LLM calls and 9% faster, but a lower KV-cache hit ratio. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00749)
- **Checklists beat self-checks in long contexts** (code-audit study): an external checklist passed 10/10 vs 5/10 for a generic self-check. — [arXiv 2607.17937](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17937)

### Inferences
- Xylo's context assembler should be an explicit, testable module (ContextPipe-style) with an auditable trace of what went into each call, why, and its token cost. Making context reproducible is a prerequisite for tuning compaction prompts ACON- or PAIR-style.
- Treat the file system as the primary long-term working store for the current task: todo.md or plan, progress notes, an artifact ledger, spilled tool outputs and a searchable transcript. The context window then holds pointers plus the recent working set.
- Recitation should be a harness feature, not a model habit. The harness re-injects the current todo and constraints block near the tail (Manus; Du et al.; Classifier Context Rot reminders).
- Maintain playbooks and summaries as itemized deltas (ACE). Wholesale regeneration loses details at each pass (ACE's context collapse; Factory's results; Compact-Bench).

### Gaps
- Most industry numbers (Anthropic +29/39%, −84%; Cursor −46.9%; OpenHands 2×) come from internal evals with limited methodology disclosure.
- I found no peer-reviewed replication of Manus's KV-cache claims beyond the cost arithmetic. "Don't Break the Cache" (Q5) is the closest academic confirmation.
- Cognition's and LangChain's context-engineering posts were not fetched. As far as I know they are mainly qualitative and do not report measured results, so they were deprioritized.

## Q5. KV-cache / prompt-caching-friendly context design (stable prefixes, append-only histories, cache breakpoints) and its relevance for local inference

### Takeaway
For agents, which are prefill-heavy (about 100:1 input:output per Manus), prompt caching dominates cost and latency. Measured savings are 41–80% of API cost and 13–31% faster time to first token (TTFT), and up to 98.6% of input tokens are cache hits in public coding trajectories. Any context-management edit that rewrites earlier tokens (masking, summarization, reordering, query-aware compression, adding or removing tools, timestamps in the system prompt) invalidates everything after it. So compaction should be **rare, batched and large**, and dynamic content belongs at the end. Several 2026 studies show that naive token reduction can *raise* billed cost because of cache misses. Local inference (llama.cpp) relies on the same prefix reuse, and the benefit is larger there because prefill on CPU or consumer GPUs is slow.

### Cited Findings

**Measured impact**
- **Don't Break the Cache: An Evaluation of Prompt Caching for Long-Horizon Agentic Tasks**: Lumer, Nizar, Jangiti, Frank et al.; arXiv 2601.06007. Over 500 sessions on DeepResearch Bench with 10K-token system prompts across OpenAI, Anthropic and Google:
  - Prompt caching cuts API cost 41–80% and improves TTFT 13–31%.
  - Strategic cache-block control (dynamic content at the *end* of the system prompt, avoiding dynamic function definitions, excluding dynamic tool results from cached blocks) is more consistent than naive full-context caching, "which can paradoxically increase latency."
  - Benefits scale linearly after the provider's minimum cacheable length (prompt sizes 500–50,000 tokens; 3–50 tool calls). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06007)
- **TurnCost: Cache-Aware Loop Engineering for Coding Agents**: arXiv 2610.15093 (preprint). 98.6% of input tokens in 600 trajectories are served from the prefix cache, so flat input pricing overestimates loop cost by about 7×. Turn count, not observation size, drives cost (84% vs 4% of variance). Delegating turns to cheaper models raised cost in half of settings, near a derived break-even boundary. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15093)
- **Token Reduction Is Not Cost Reduction**: arXiv 2607.12161 (preprint). A 38.4% reduction in tool-output tokens gave +6.8% billed cost, because cache creation and reads dominate input cost. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12161)
- **Cache-Aware Prompt Compression (CAPC): A Two-Tier Cost Model for LLM API Caching**: Yan Song; arXiv 2607.15516 (preprint). On Anthropic Sonnet 4.6, the measured cache had a sharp threshold near 3,500 tokens, below which the hit rate plateaus at about 0.83 over 30-call sessions. Query-aware compression invalidates the cache on every call. Query-*agnostic* compression plus explicit `cache_control` was cheapest in 16/16 LongBench-v2 configurations: 49% cheaper than cache-only, 64% cheaper than query-aware compression and 90% cheaper than vanilla, with quality within 0.05. On tau-bench retail it matched vanilla reward (36/50) at the lowest cost, while query-aware compression cost +40.1% over vanilla. On a production assistant with a 94K-token schema prefix it saved 51.7%. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15516)
- **CADOC: Cache-Aware Dynamic Object Context**: Yao, Zhou, Xu; arXiv 2609.37012 (preprint). Structured objects are replaced with compact retrievable "Cards," but the replacements are *scheduled in batches* by trading accumulated waiting cost against the shared cost of rebuilding the cache (an economic-order-quantity rule). This cut input cost about 40% on average with near-full-context task performance. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37012)
- **Silent and Costly: Prompt-Caching Mis-Integration in LLM Agents**: Zhang, Guo, Liu, Deng et al.; arXiv 2610.34817; ICSE 2027. Across 120 caching-related PRs in 10 open-source agent systems, mis-integrations (request-side misconfiguration, read-side usage miscounting) mostly "fail silently." A linter found 32 in 571 projects (precision 0.86), leading to 18 upstream fixes, including one in Anthropic's SDK. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34817)
- **ICLR reasoning pruning** cut cache-read tokens 33.3% (arXiv 2609.29875). Pruning reasoning in history interacts with caching as well. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29875)

**Provider mechanics (current docs, fetched 2026-10-31)**
- **Anthropic prompt caching:**
  - Pricing: 5-minute cache writes at 1.25× base input, 1-hour writes at 2×, reads at 0.1× by default (0.05× for Opus 5.5 / Sonnet 5.5; 0.025× for Fable/Mythos 5.1).
  - Minimum cacheable length varies by model (512 / 1,024 / 2,048 / 4,096 tokens). Shorter prompts are silently not cached.
  - Up to 4 explicit breakpoints. Lookback is 20 positions per breakpoint, and runs of consecutive tool_use or tool_result blocks count as one position.
  - Hierarchy is tools → system → messages, and a change at one level invalidates that level and all after it. Changing tool definitions invalidates everything. `tool_choice` and image add/remove invalidate system and messages. Thinking parameters invalidate messages.
  - Automatic caching (a top-level `cache_control`) moves the breakpoint forward as the conversation grows. The 5-minute TTL refreshes on each use. — [Claude docs: prompt caching](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching)
- **Anthropic context editing:** "Tool result clearing invalidates cached prefixes when content is cleared." Use `clear_at_least` so each clear justifies the cache write. Keeping thinking blocks preserves the cache. — [Claude docs: context editing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/context-editing)
- **OpenAI prompt caching:**
  - Minimum 1,024 tokens (GPT-5.6+); older models use implicit breakpoints every 2,048 tokens (GPT-5.5) or at model-dependent intervals.
  - Reads cost 0.1× (0.05× on some GPT-6.1 tiers). Writes cost 1.25× for 30 minutes, or 2.5× for 12 hours in the Responses API.
  - Routing hashes the initial tokens, including tool definitions, plus an optional `prompt_cache_key`. Traffic above about 15 requests per minute can overflow to other machines.
  - Guidance: stable content first; timestamps and dynamic data last; append rather than rewrite, since "summarization, compaction, or truncation can reset reuse"; keep tool definitions and order stable; use `tool_choice`/`allowed_tools` instead of removing tools. — [OpenAI docs: prompt caching](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/prompt-caching)
- **OpenAI compaction:** server-side `context_management` with `compact_threshold` (the example uses 200,000) emits an encrypted, opaque compaction item in-stream. The standalone `/responses/compact` endpoint returns a compacted window that should be passed through unpruned. — [OpenAI docs: compaction](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/compaction)

**Local inference (llama.cpp / Ollama)**
- **llama.cpp server** (current README):
  - `cache_prompt` (default true) reuses the KV cache from the previous request so only the differing suffix is processed. Logits may not be bit-identical across batch sizes, so it "can cause nondeterministic results."
  - `--cache-reuse N` / `n_cache_reuse`: minimum chunk size for reuse "via KV shifting" (default 0, disabled). This lets a prompt with an edited middle reuse later chunks.
  - `--keep N` / `n_keep`: tokens to keep from the initial prompt when context is exceeded. `--context-shift` is disabled by default.
  - `--cache-ram` is a host-memory prompt cache (default 8,192 MiB). `--cache-idle-slots` saves idle slots to it.
  - `--ctx-checkpoints` (default 32 per slot) and `--checkpoint-min-step` (default 8,192 tokens) are context checkpoints.
  - `--slot-prompt-similarity` (default 0.10) sets how similar a prompt must be to reuse a slot. `--slot-save-path` persists slot KV. `-np/--parallel` sets slots, and `--kv-unified` shares a KV buffer. — [llama.cpp server README](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/server/README.md)
- **Prompt Cache: Modular Attention Reuse for Low-Latency Inference**: Gim, Chen, Lee, Sarda et al.; arXiv 2311.04934; MLSys 2024. Reusing precomputed attention states for repeated prompt modules (system messages, templates, documents) cuts TTFT 8× on GPU and 60× on CPU. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04934)
- **SGLang (RadixAttention)**: Zheng, Yin, Xie, Sun et al.; arXiv 2312.07104. Radix-tree KV reuse gives up to 6.4× throughput on agent, multi-turn and RAG workloads. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.07104)
- **KVMem: Virtualizing Million-Token Agent Workspaces on a Consumer GPU**: Chai, Wang, Su, Xia et al.; arXiv 2609.04852 (preprint). Overflowed history is kept as paged KV (GPU, host, NVMe) and selected by attention-space indexes. On DeepSWE with Qwen3.8-27B, success rose from 43.8% (compaction-only) to 48.4%. It runs workspaces up to 1M tokens (4× the native 256K) on a 24 GB laptop RTX 5090 at about 50 tokens/s. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04852)
- **What Does a Harness Buy?**: "The provider's price for cached input scales the bill and does not reorder it." The preamble times the step count sets the cost. — [arXiv 2610.04433](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04433)

### Inferences
- **Prefix discipline for Xylo:**
  - Use a fixed order: tool schemas (sorted, deterministic JSON) → system prompt (no timestamps or volatile data) → stable project rules and memory → history (append-only) → volatile block (date/time, git status, open files, todo recitation) at the tail.
  - Never re-serialize earlier messages differently. Store the exact bytes sent and replay them.
  - Restrict tools by masking (`tool_choice`/`allowed_tools`, or llama.cpp grammar/logit constraints) rather than editing the tool list.
- **Compaction as a cache event:**
  - Every mask or summary edit is a cache bust from the edit point onward. Batch edits (Anthropic `clear_at_least`; the CADOC EOQ rule) so each bust frees a large chunk, for example clearing at least 20–30% of the window.
  - Prefer editing the *oldest* region so the rebuilt prefix is short.
  - Put the compaction summary right after the stable prefix so it becomes the new long-lived cached segment.
- **Measure what matters:** log provider cache metrics (cached vs. uncached input tokens per call) and compute cost per *successful task*, not tokens per request (Factory, TurnCost, Token Reduction ≠ Cost). Add a CI check for cache-hit regressions, since mis-integrations are silent (ICSE 2027 study).
- **Local path:**
  - Keep one llama.cpp slot per agent session (slot affinity via slot id and prompt similarity) with `cache_prompt` on.
  - Use `--cache-reuse` (e.g., 256-token chunks) when the harness edits the middle of history.
  - Set `--keep` to the system-prompt length if context shift is ever enabled.
  - Size `--cache-ram` for multiple concurrent sessions.
  - On CPU or small GPUs, a cache miss on a 30K prompt can cost tens of seconds of prefill (Prompt Cache's 60× CPU result), so the bias toward rare, batched compaction is even stronger locally than on cloud APIs.
- **Hybrid cloud plus local:** a local model doing tool-output triage or compression before the cloud call (Local-Splitter) must still produce deterministic outputs, or it will break the cloud model's prefix cache.

### Gaps
- No public benchmark measured llama.cpp `--cache-reuse` or context checkpoints on agent workloads. Their impact on Xylo-like workloads is unmeasured.
- Gemini's implicit and explicit caching mechanics were not fetched. "Don't Break the Cache" covers Google but I did not extract provider-specific numbers.
- CAPC's two-tier cache threshold (~3,500 tokens, ρ≈0.83) is one author's measurement on one model, and may change with provider updates.

## Q6. Token budgeting: allocating budget across system prompt, tools, memory, history and tool outputs; when to trigger compaction; handling models with small windows

### Takeaway
Published defaults cluster around triggering tool-result clearing or compaction at about 100K tokens for 200K-class cloud models (Anthropic) or a configurable threshold (OpenAI example 200K). The fixed preamble (system prompt plus tool schemas) is the dominant cost multiplier, sometimes 21K–57K tokens per turn, and must be budgeted first. For small windows (8K–49K), evidence says to (1) compact *early*, leaving room for a full reasoning turn plus output, since running out of room mid-reasoning is the main failure; (2) use small fixed thresholds such as ACON's (observation >1K, history >4K); (3) expect each local summary to cost minutes, not seconds; and (4) raise Ollama's default context (4K on machines with under 24 GiB VRAM) to at least 64K for agent work when memory allows, or design for the small window explicitly.

### Cited Findings
- **Anthropic API defaults:** context-editing trigger at 100,000 input tokens, keeping the 3 most recent tool uses. SDK compaction `context_token_threshold` is 100,000. Server-side compaction (`compact_20260112`) is recommended. — [Claude docs: context editing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/context-editing)
- **OpenAI compaction:** `compact_threshold` set by the developer (documented example 200,000), with no recommended value. — [OpenAI docs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/compaction)
- **Anthropic research agent:** saves its plan to memory because the context "will be truncated" past 200,000 tokens. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)
- **Claude "context awareness":** newer Claude models track their remaining token budget. The docs suggest telling the model that context will be auto-compacted so it does not stop early, and that it should save progress to memory as it nears the limit. — [Claude docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices)
- **ACON thresholds:** history compression above 4,096 tokens and observation compression above 1,024 tokens gave the best trade-off. Peak tokens on AppWorld were about 7.3K with ACON versus 9.9K uncompressed. — [arXiv 2510.00615](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.00615v3)
- **JetBrains settings:** observation-masking window M=10 turns. Summarization of N=21 turns keeping M=10 (first summary at turn 31). The hybrid used N=43, W=10, and untuned N made it worse. — [arXiv 2508.21433](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.21433v3)
- **Small-window evidence (Bud ContextCompact 2.0):** compaction to a 16K budget beat both truncation and the *full 32K history* for a 1.2B model (0.335 vs 0.247 vs 0.176). With a 49K window and a 27B reasoning model, compaction gave +12.3 points on long sessions. Without compaction, the dominant failure was "a reasoning turn exhausting the room the history left." The gain appeared "only when compaction triggered early enough to leave room." Median 161 s per summary; the gain vanished under a 45-minute cap. An 8B model finished after about 10 turns at 5–8K tokens and never needed compaction. — [arXiv 2610.25516](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25516)
- **Threshold sensitivity depends on window size:** for 1M-context models the compaction threshold "does not measurably affect" performance, but the smallest-window model (GPT-5.3-Codex) did worse at lower thresholds. — [arXiv 2610.31778](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31778)
- **The value of context management grows as budgets tighten**, mainly through preventing overflow failures. Rule-based elision before LLM summarization is the most efficient. — [arXiv 2609.20804](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20804)
- **Under a hard 32K limit, compressing old observations beats full text** (21.1% vs 11.1% for Qwen3-4B, with soft tokens and a trained model). — [arXiv 2609.31430](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31430)
- **The preamble dominates cost:** tool-schema blocks of about 21K–57K tokens per turn; harness cost differs up to 3×, set by the system prompt plus tool schemas times the step count. — [arXiv 2609.22114](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22114); [arXiv 2610.04433](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04433)
- **Instruction density:** at most 68% accuracy at 500 instructions, with bias toward earlier ones. — [arXiv 2507.11538](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11538)
- **Effective length of local models** (RULER): Llama3.1-8B 32K, Qwen3-8B 64K (self-reported), Mistral-7B-v0.2 16K, Mistral-Nemo 16K, Phi3-mini 32K, Qwen3-4B 64K (self-reported). — [RULER](https://github.com/NVIDIA/RULER)
- **Ollama defaults:** context length depends on VRAM: 4K under 24 GiB, 32K for 24–48 GiB, 256K for 48 GiB or more. Cloud models default to their maximum. Change it via `OLLAMA_CONTEXT_LENGTH` or the app slider. "Tasks which require large context like web search, agents, and coding tools should be set to at least 64000 tokens." — [Ollama docs](https://docs.ollama.com/context-length)
- **Output caps matter:** one harness's deficit was partly runs cut short by its output cap. — [arXiv 2610.04433](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04433)
- **Sub-agent return budget:** "often 1,000–2,000 tokens." — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)
- **Context management vs. latency:** compressed policies using about one third the tokens took 20–80% longer on Terminal-Bench (Qwen). — [arXiv 2609.32961](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32961)

### Inferences
These are proposed budgeting rules for Xylo, synthesized from the above and not directly measured.
- **Define W_eff** = min(nominal `n_ctx`, configured Ollama or llama.cpp context, RULER-style effective length for the model family). Reserve R = max_output + reasoning allowance. For reasoning models on small windows, reserve generously (8K+), since Bud's main failure was the reasoning turn running out of room.
- **Fixed preamble P** (system plus tool schemas plus pinned rules): keep it at or below about 15–20% of W_eff for small-window models. Defer rarely used tool schemas (names only, details loaded on demand, as with Cursor's MCP folders). Keep project instruction files short (IFScale).
- **Pinned block:** original task, user constraints verbatim, current todo. Never summarized. Re-injected at the tail.
- **Triggers:**
  - Tier-1 observation masking once total exceeds about 50–60% of (W_eff − R). Mask in batches.
  - Tier-2 summarization once total exceeds about 75–80% of (W_eff − R) after masking, with a target of about 30–40% after compaction.
  - For 1M-window cloud models the exact threshold matters little (Salle et al.). Pick thresholds to manage cost, and cache economics, not quality.
  - For small windows, trigger *early*.
- **Per-object caps:** single tool observation above about 1K tokens (ACON) or above about 10–20% of W_eff gets spilled to a file with a head/tail preview. Search results capped at about 50 (SWE-agent). File views windowed at about 100 lines (SWE-agent).
- **Small-model specifics:**
  - Prefer branch-and-return sub-agents, where each sub-task gets a fresh window and returns a 1–2K-token report (THREAD showed large gains for 7–8B models).
  - Run summarization on the same local model only if latency allows (161 s per summary on local hardware). Otherwise use a smaller local compressor, or a cloud model when the user permits.
  - Detect Ollama's actual `num_ctx` at runtime rather than assuming the model card's value.

### Gaps
- No study systematically sweeps compaction-trigger thresholds for 8K–32K local models on agentic tasks. Bud ContextCompact gives one data point (16K and 49K) and a qualitative "trigger early" finding.
- Ollama's docs do not say whether over-length prompts are truncated silently or rejected. Xylo should verify this empirically.
- No source gives a validated budget split (percent for system, tools, history, outputs). The percentages above are design heuristics.

## Q7. Retrieval vs. long context: when is RAG over history better than stuffing the context?

### Takeaway
With unlimited budget, long context (LC) usually beats chunk-RAG on average quality (Self-Route). Retrieval is far cheaper, gets the same answer on about 63% of queries, and helps even long-context models (NVIDIA). Small-window models gain the most from retrieval. For agent *history*, the best 2025–26 systems are hybrids: a compact working context plus a lossless external log the agent can query by pointer, grep or code (Cursor history files, Memex, Scroll, RLMs). Routing (try the cheap or small context first, escalate when "unanswerable") is a practical harness policy. Retrieval has its own failure mode for some models: answering from the wrong file ("evidence substitution"), which explicit pointers to the authoritative output mitigate.

### Cited Findings
- **Retrieval meets Long Context Large Language Models**: Xu, Ping, Wu, McAfee et al. (NVIDIA); arXiv 2310.03025; ICLR 2024. A 4K-context LLM with simple retrieval matched a 16K fine-tuned LLM (positional interpolation) on long-context tasks with much less computation. Retrieval improved models "regardless of their extended context window sizes." Retrieval-augmented Llama2-70B-32K beat GPT-3.5-turbo-16k and Davinci003 on average over nine tasks, and beat its own non-retrieval 32K baseline while generating faster. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03025)
- **Retrieval Augmented Generation or Long-Context LLMs? A Comprehensive Study and Hybrid Approach (Self-Route)**: Li, Li, Zhang, Mei et al.; arXiv 2407.16833; EMNLP 2024 Industry. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.16833); [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.16833v2)
  - Average scores across nine datasets (LC / RAG / Self-Route): Gemini-1.5-Pro 49.70 / 37.33 / 46.41; GPT-4o 48.67 / 32.60 / 48.89; GPT-3.5-Turbo 32.07 / 30.33 / 35.32.
  - Self-Route's tokens relative to LC: 38.39% (Gemini), 61.40% (GPT-4o), 38.85% (GPT-3.5). The paper cites cost reductions of 65% and 39%.
  - RAG and LC predictions are identical for 63% of queries.
  - Procedure: RAG first (k=5 chunks of about 300 words), with the model allowed to answer "unanswerable." Only those queries go to full LC.
  - Inconsistency: the paper's text and tables disagree slightly (e.g., the 65% figure vs. a token share implying about 62%), so cite the table values.
- **LongRAG: Enhancing RAG with Long-context LLMs**: Jiang, Ma, Chen; arXiv 2406.15319. Retrieval units are 4K tokens (grouped related docs) with fewer than 8 units retrieved. Without training it reaches 62.7% EM on NQ and 64.3% on HotpotQA, on par with fully trained state of the art. Whole-document units gave 25.9% F1 on Qasper and 57.5% on MultiFieldQA-en. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.15319)
  - Harness note: when retrieving from history or files, retrieve coherent large units (a whole tool call plus result, a whole file section) rather than tiny chunks.
- **Lost in the Middle:** going from 20 to 50 retrieved docs added only about 1–1.5%, and reader accuracy saturates before recall. — [arXiv 2307.03172](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.03172v3)
- **Chroma:** focused ~300-token prompts beat full ~113K-token LongMemEval prompts for every model. — [Chroma](https://www.trychroma.com/research/context-rot)
- **LongMemEval:** about 30% accuracy drop for long-context LLMs memorizing across sustained interactions. — [arXiv 2410.10813](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10813)
- **HELMET:** RAG tasks are recommended as a fast proxy that better predicts downstream long-context performance. — [arXiv 2410.02694](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02694)
- **Recursive Language Models:** treating the long input as an environment explored by code and recursive sub-calls beat compaction by a median 26% on GPT-5, at comparable cost. — [arXiv 2512.24601](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24601)
- **Scroll (Context as an Environment):** an append-only event log plus a Python kernel. Only printed projections enter the context, and evicted spans keep landmarks to exact log addresses. — [arXiv 2608.21690](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21690)
- **Memex(RL):** indexed external experience memory with dereferenceable indices. It is "substantially less lossy" than summary-only approaches. — [arXiv 2603.04257](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04257)
- **Cursor:** after summarization, the agent gets a reference to its chat-history file and searches it for details missing from the summary. — [Cursor](https://cursor.com/blog/dynamic-context-discovery)
- **Same Simulation, Different Evidence:** retrieval via a section index cost GPT-5.1 and GPT-5.2 11.3 and 7.9 points but cost Claude 4.6 models nothing. Naming the authoritative output recovered 6.3–7.9 points, and inlining outputs that fit worked as well. — [arXiv 2610.26836](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26836)
- **Compression vs. re-acquisition:** dropped state is re-fetched through extra retrieval calls (21.0 to 63.9 for GPT-5.5) without changing completion. — [arXiv 2608.16370](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16370)
- **KVMem (local):** retrieving relevant *KV blocks* of overflowed history beat compaction-only (43.8% to 48.4% on DeepSWE). — [arXiv 2609.04852](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04852)
- **Claude docs:** starting a fresh window and letting the model rediscover state from the filesystem (progress.txt, tests.json, git log) can beat compaction. — [Claude docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices)

### Inferences
- **For Xylo's session history:** keep (a) the recent raw window, (b) a structured compacted summary, and (c) the full transcript and tool outputs on disk with stable IDs. Give the agent a `search_history`/`read_history(id)` tool, or plain grep over a transcript file. This combines LC quality for recent work with RAG cost for old work, and makes compaction recoverable.
- **Self-Route policy for small local models:** answer from the compact context first. If the model signals missing information (e.g., "need details from step 37"), dereference the pointer rather than re-inflating the whole history.
- **Placeholders should name the authoritative source** and how to read it (path, line range, command). For models known to substitute evidence (some GPT versions in the PHREEQC study), inline small outputs instead of spilling them.
- **Retrieve larger coherent units** (whole tool turns, whole functions) rather than tiny chunks (LongRAG).

### Gaps
- No study directly compares "RAG over agent history" with "compaction" with "full history" for coding agents on frontier closed models with cost and cache effects included. Scroll, Memex, ACM and KVMem each use their own (often trained or open) models.
- Self-Route-style routing has not been evaluated on agent *histories*, only on document QA.
- Whether frontier 1M-token models (Gemini 3.x, Claude 5.x) have narrowed the LC-vs-RAG gap on agent histories in 2026 is not established. The Classifier Context Rot paper (misses 2–30× more often after 800K tokens) suggests they have not at extreme lengths.
